# Case study: Binance hot wallet outflow, 29 October 2026

> Status at the time of writing (30 Oct 2026): Binance has **not confirmed** a hack and says it is
> investigating "reports of a potential security issue". The figures below come from public on-chain
> analysis and press reports and may be revised.

## Public timeline

| Time (UTC) | Event |
|---|---|
| 28–29 Oct | The address that triggered the withdrawals receives gas through **Tornado Cash** (three deposits of ~0.1 ETH) |
| 20:24:23 | One transaction from *Binance 14*: ~167,464 ETH plus **63 ERC-20 tokens**, including ~1.6B USDT, to an address that did not exist before 29 Oct |
| +24 s | ~28,000 ETH from *Binance 16* to the **same** address |
| 20:41:23 | ~780M USDC from *Hot Wallet 34* to the same address |
| 20:24–21:04 | Main outflow over ~40 minutes; estimates range from $2.5B to $5.6B |
| Evening | Tether and Circle freeze a large part of the USDT / USDC |

## Important caveat

VaultSentinel protects the **signing path**. If an attacker extracts a private key and signs outside the
pipeline, no pre-signing filter will see the transaction. That is why VaultSentinel must be a mandatory
co-signer in an MPC / multisig scheme (see [INTEGRATION.md](INTEGRATION.md)) so that no valid signature
can exist without its attestation.
